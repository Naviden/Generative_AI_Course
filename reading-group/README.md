# PhD reading group — three lessons

This is a separate discussion path. The existing course lessons, theory reference, labs and archive retain their current structure.

**The first meeting needs no advance reading or installation.** Start with Lesson 01 and work through its basic concepts and examples together. The other two meetings build on that common vocabulary. Each lesson contains its own explanations, worked calculations, discussion prompts and record of conclusions. You can complete the group discussion on that page without visiting the long theory file, lab workbook, checkpoint answers or a separate worksheet.

| Meeting | Open one page | Central question | What the group leaves with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Shared foundations](01-foundations.md) | What does it mean to learn a distribution? | Shared definitions of probability, loss, sampling, representations and evidence; a list of unresolved concepts |
| 2 | [How generative models work](02-generative-models.md) | What does a tractable objective tell us about a generator? | Worked VAE, diffusion and flow examples; an explicit distinction between model, inference, solver and measurement errors |
| 3 | [Preferences and research evidence](03-preferences-and-evidence.md) | What supports a claim beyond fitting training labels? | A worked DPO interpretation, a controlled critique and a testable research proposal |

## How to use these pages together

Read an explanation together, follow its numerical example, then discuss the adjacent question using the worked response. Invite someone to explain the reasoning and someone else to challenge an assumption. Record a supported claim, its conditions, an unresolved objection and the next useful measurement. Rotate contributions naturally; no fixed roles, grading scheme or pre-meeting homework is required.

Lesson 01 is deliberately a foundations meeting. Later lessons introduce the harder mathematics where it is used. Derivations are learning material to work through together, not an entrance test. The optional reverse-SDE derivation in Lesson 02 is expandable; the main path does not depend on opening it.

Paper links appear at the end of each lesson for verification or deeper reading. Use them to check an original argument or examine its empirical evidence. Optional computer demonstrations are complete on the relevant lesson page, including expected answers, commands, output interpretation and limitations. If running them, use the existing [installation guide](../docs/setup.md) once; installation is unnecessary for the first meeting and for the worked discussions.

All numerical examples labelled illustrative or hypothetical are supplied reasoning exercises. They are not measurements from the cited papers. If the group runs an experiment, record its actual configuration and outputs and keep its conclusion within the evidence.
